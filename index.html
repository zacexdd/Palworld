What Is Palworld Mobile?

Palworld Mobile is the official mobile adaptation of Palworld, developed by Krafton's PUBG Studios under a licensing agreement with Pocketpair. Unlike many mobile ports, Krafton has confirmed the game will not use a live-service model, and aims to faithfully carry over the core Pal-collecting, base-building, and survival systems from the original PC and console release.

The mobile version was first revealed publicly at G-STAR 2025 in South Korea, showing a new skill-based strategic battle system built specifically for mobile controls, alongside social features like Pal breeding in a shared Plaza and cooperative or competitive boss battles.

Palworld Mobile for Android: What We Know

Palworld Mobile is being developed by Krafton's PUBG Studios, the same team behind the Android port of PUBG Mobile, which launched via Google Play with regular content parity to console/PC. That's the expected distribution model here too — a standard Play Store release, not a sideloaded APK.

Unconfirmed so far: minimum Android OS version, RAM requirements, and storage size. The mobile build is expected to include scaled-down graphics and asset streaming to run on a wider range of Android hardware than the original PC version requires.

Confirmed for Android: the game will not use a live-service or battle-pass model, matching the PC version's single-purchase structure.

Closed alpha testing so far has been limited to Japan and South Korea, and it's unclear whether that alpha included Android devices, iOS devices, or both.

Palworld Mobile for iOS: What We Know

An iOS release is expected alongside Android, based on Krafton's framing of Palworld Mobile as a cross-platform release. No App Store listing, TestFlight beta, or iOS-specific release window has been announced yet.

One iOS-specific factor worth watching: Apple's App Store review process for games with loot-based creature capture and online features tends to be stricter than Google Play's, particularly around age ratings and monetization disclosures. This could mean iOS timing lags slightly behind Android even under the same broad launch window.

iOS device compatibility hasn't been announced. As with Android, there is no legitimate iOS build, TestFlight link, or IPA file available anywhere right now.
